\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we formalized and empirically investigated the problem of rollout-free competence boundary forecasting in visual reinforcement learning.
Across a 6,600-episode benchmark on continuous visual control, our goal has been to document transparently both the predictive power and empirical boundaries of pre-rollout forecasting signals.

\subsection{What the Data Supports}
Our findings provide concrete empirical evidence for three key conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Latent Feature Early-Warning Discrimination:} In continuous visual control, $k$NN in the policy's latent representation space reliably discriminates impending failure under strict holdout (AUROC $0.912$, AUPRC $0.949$), significantly outperforming raw-pixel $k$NN ($0.834$) and random projections ($0.806$), and achieving an absolute forecast error of $0.083$ severity units (median $0.017$).
    \item \textbf{Policy Ranking via Action Disagreement:} Policy-conditional signals effectively rank policy robustness across shift families: Action Disagreement achieves Kendall $\tau = 0.581$ (selection regret $0.032$) and $Q$-value drop achieves $\tau = 0.513$ (regret $0.023$), whereas shift-agnostic scores fail to separate policies (Proposition~1).
    \item \textbf{Prevalence Mixture Portability:} The analytical AUROC mixture formula (Proposition~2) predicts empirical failure-detection AUROC across arbitrary deployment regime mixes with a mean absolute error of only $0.010$ across 105 mixture test cases.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the Data Does Not Support}
Equally importantly, our experimental results disprove several prevalent assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Abrupt Failure Shape Myth ($H_1$):} Contrary to the widespread assumption that visual RL policies collapse abruptly like cliffs, only $44.0\%$ of evaluated curves (11 of 25) display cliffness $\ge 0.5$. Performance degradation in continuous control is predominantly gradual.
    \item \textbf{Test-Time Self-Supervised Adaptation Benefit ($H_6$):} In 40-episode paired comparisons, test-time self-supervision (TTSS) achieves no statistically significant improvement over unadapted policies (all Holm-adjusted $p = 1.0$). While the trust-region safeguard actively rejects corrupted updates under severe noise ($100\%$ rejected), adaptation fails to restore task performance once representations drift beyond the training distribution.
    \item \textbf{Conformal False Alarm Calibration ($H_7$):} Clean-only conformal thresholding targeting $\alpha = 0.05$ yielded an empirical false alarm rate of $0.117$ on clean test episodes, demonstrating the difficulty of extreme-tail quantile calibration without shifted calibration data.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single Primary Environment:} The 6,600-episode continuous sweep was conducted on the Visual Pursuit tracking benchmark. Replicating the full $s^*$ boundary forecasting sweep across multi-joint manipulation and locomotion domains is an essential next step.
    \item \textbf{Modern Baselines (TACO and D.Va):} We implemented and trained the Temporal Action-Contrastive (TACO) baseline architecture to competence on Visual Pursuit (30,000 steps, clean mean CLE $4.57$\,px, \texttt{results/taco\_summary.json}); however, scaling multi-seed 6,600-episode training sweeps across all 5 shift axes was deferred due to compute budget constraints. Benchmarking against differentiable dynamics methods (D.Va) was precluded because Visual Pursuit dynamics are non-differentiable through standard simulator step functions.
    \item \textbf{Censored Boundary Extrapolation:} Exactly 9 of 30 curves were censored at the maximal tested severity ($s^* = 1.0$), particularly under viewpoint affine shifts where policies remain robust. Investigating unconstrained severity manifolds is left for future exploration.
\end{itemize}
