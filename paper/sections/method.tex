\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

In this section, we present the mathematical and algorithmic formulation of our visual representation metrics, geometric manifold embeddings, and test-time adaptation framework.

\subsection{Environment and Problem Setting}
\label{sec:method:env}

We evaluate policies in a visual continuous control benchmark: \textbf{Visual Pursuit}. 
In this domain, the agent receives high-dimensional pixel observations $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64 \times 3}$ and emits continuous 2D velocity actions $a_t = (\Delta x, \Delta y) \in [-1, 1]^2$ to control a tracking reticle toward a dynamically moving target on a 2D plane.
The instantaneous task reward is the negative Centre-of-target Localization Error (CLE):
\begin{equation}
    r_t = - \|p_{\mathrm{reticle}, t} - p_{\mathrm{target}, t}\|_2.
\end{equation}
An episode is classified as successful if the episode-mean CLE remains strictly below $5.0$\,pixels (whereas an untrained random policy produces a baseline tracking error of $\approx 22.0$\,px).

We evaluate visual policies trained with DrQ-v2~\citep{yarats2021drqv2} and SVEA~\citep{hansen2021svea} across three perturbation regimes:
photometric shifts (brightness, contrast, hue variations), sensor camera noise, and severe compounding shifts (simultaneous contrast degradation, color inversion, and Gaussian noise).

\subsection{Classical Representation Distance \texorpdfstring{$d_\mathrm{rep}$}{d\_rep}}
\label{sec:method:drep}

Let $\phi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^D$ denote a visual feature encoder mapping raw images to $D$-dimensional latent vectors. Given an offline clean observation distribution $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{clean}}$ and an unlabelled target observation distribution $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pert}}$, the classical representation drift is defined as the mean pairwise Euclidean distance:
\begin{equation}
    d_\mathrm{rep}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{clean}}, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pert}}) = \mathbb{E}_{(x, x') \sim (\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{clean}}, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pert}})} \left[ \|\phi(x) - \phi(x')\|_2 \right].
\end{equation}
For paired trajectories collected along identical action or target traces, $d_\mathrm{rep}$ measures the temporal mean distance between clean and perturbed frame embeddings: $\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T \|\phi(x_t^{\mathrm{clean}}) - \phi(x_t^{\mathrm{pert}})\|_2$.

\subsection{Semantic Distance Weighting \texorpdfstring{$\tilde{d}_\mathrm{rep}$}{d-tilde\_rep}}
\label{sec:method:semantic}

A key vulnerability of classical $d_\mathrm{rep}$ is that background distractors (e.g., changes in sky or wall color) create substantial Euclidean feature shifts despite having zero impact on optimal policy execution.
To decouple task-relevant feature changes from task-irrelevant appearance changes, we introduce spatial semantic weighting.

Let $\phi_{\mathrm{spatial}}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H' \times W'}$ denote unpooled spatial feature maps before final pooling. 
A semantic weighter backend generates a normalized spatial weight matrix $W_s \in [0, 1]^{H' \times W'}$ where pixels deemed critical for decision-making receive weights close to $1.0$, while task-irrelevant background regions receive a floor weight $\beta \in [0, 1]$ (set to $\beta = 0.1$ in our implementation).
The semantically-weighted representation distance is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{d}_\mathrm{rep}(x, x', W_s) = \frac{\sum_{h=1}^{H'} \sum_{w=1}^{W'} W_{s, h, w} \, \|\phi_{\mathrm{spatial}}(x)_{:, h, w} - \phi_{\mathrm{spatial}}(x')_{:, h, w}\|_2}{\sum_{h=1}^{H'} \sum_{w=1}^{W'} W_{s, h, w}}.
\end{equation}
We evaluate three weighter formulations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SegmentationWeighter (GT Mask):} Utilizes ground-truth entity segmentation masks from the simulator. Regions containing active targets or hazards are assigned weight $1.0$, and non-entity pixels are assigned baseline floor weight $\beta$.
    \item \textbf{TLDAWeighter (Lipschitz Policy):} Estimates the local Lipschitz sensitivity of policy actions with respect to spatial image locations via target-layer discriminant analysis~\citep{yuan2022tlda}.
    \item \textbf{SaliencyWeighter (Critic Gradient):} Computes gradient saliency of the state-action value critic $Q_\theta(s, a)$ with respect to spatial feature activations: $W_s \propto \|\nabla_{\phi_{\mathrm{spatial}}} Q_\theta(s, a)\|_1$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Grassmann Subspace Manifold Analysis}
\label{sec:method:grassmann}

Rather than tracking single-frame point embeddings, we analyze the geometric geometry of entire trajectory subspaces on the Grassmann manifold $\mathrm{Gr}(k, D)$, the set of all $k$-dimensional linear subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^D$~\citep{bendokat2020grassmann,huang2016riemannian}.

Given a trajectory activation matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times D}$ of $T$ time steps, we center the features and compute the truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD):
\begin{equation}
    A - \bar{A} = U \Sigma V^\top.
\end{equation}
The first $k$ columns of $V$ (or left singular vectors of covariance) form an orthonormal basis $U_k \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times k}$ representing a point $[U_k] \in \mathrm{Gr}(k, D)$ satisfying $U_k^\top U_k = I_k$.

Given two subspaces $U_1, U_2 \in \mathrm{Gr}(k, D)$ representing clean and perturbed trajectories, we compute the singular values of their mutual projection:
\begin{equation}
    U_1^\top U_2 = Y \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_k) Z^\top,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_i = \cos(\theta_i)$ and $\theta_1 \le \theta_2 \le \cdots \le \theta_k \in [0, \pi/2]$ are the canonical \emph{principal angles} between the two subspaces.
The Riemannian geodesic distance along the manifold is:
\begin{equation}
    d_\mathrm{geo}(U_1, U_2) = \|\Theta\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^k \theta_i^2}.
\end{equation}
Complementarily, the projection distance is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    d_\mathrm{proj}(U_1, U_2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \|U_1 U_1^\top - U_2 U_2^\top\|_F = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^k \sin^2(\theta_i)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Test-Time Self-Supervised Adaptation (TTSS)}
\label{sec:method:ttss}

To mitigate performance degradation online without true reward signals, we implement a Test-Time Self-Supervised Adaptation module (\texttt{ttss/adapter.py}).
During policy deployment, an observation buffer maintains a rolling window of recent test observations $X_{\mathrm{win}} = (x_{t-W+1}, \ldots, x_t)$ of size $W = 16$.

\paragraph{Self-Supervised Adaptation Objective.}
At each control step, candidate encoder parameters $\phi'$ are obtained by performing gradient steps on a combined self-supervised objective:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{TTSS}(\phi; X_{\mathrm{win}}) = \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{smooth}(\phi; X_{\mathrm{win}}) + \lambda_{\mathrm{mom}} \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{moment}(\phi; X_{\mathrm{win}}),
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\mathrm{mom}} = 1.0$. The temporal consistency loss $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{smooth}$ penalizes erratic jumps between consecutive representations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{smooth} = \frac{1}{W-1} \sum_{i=1}^{W-1} \|\phi(x_{i+1}) - \phi(x_i)\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
The moment-matching loss $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{moment}$ aligns the test distribution's batch statistics with stored offline training statistics $(\mu_{\mathrm{off}}, \sigma^2_{\mathrm{off}})$~\citep{liu2021tttplusplus}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{moment} = \|\mu_{\mathrm{test}} - \mu_{\mathrm{off}}\|_2^2 + \|\sigma^2_{\mathrm{test}} - \sigma^2_{\mathrm{off}}\|_2^2.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Trust-Region Safeguard.}
To prevent gradient updates from corrupting well-functioning representations, candidate encoder weights $\phi'$ are evaluated before commit.
The candidate update is accepted if and only if:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{TTSS}(\phi'; X_{\mathrm{win}}) < \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{TTSS}(\phi; X_{\mathrm{win}}).
\end{equation}
If the candidate loss fails to strictly decrease, the update is rejected and the encoder state is restored to $\phi$.
In our implementation, candidate updates are generated via two iterations of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with learning rate $\eta = 10^{-4}$ and momentum $0.5$.
